The UCD Challenge — Facilitator brief

Read this together before you begin

Scenario context

A new viral infection has been detected in the UK. Your team has been tasked with developing a digital health intervention that lets members of the public report symptoms, view the latest health advice, and receive AI-generated advice tailored to their available data points: location, contacts, and wearable health data.

There is pressure to release a fully functional product quickly as this is a live public health emergency. But the team must also be mindful of the quality of what goes out under public health branding, and the significant privacy and ethical implications of the data being used.

Your mission

Deliver this service through five phases — Discovery, Alpha, Beta, Live, and Review — while keeping two things in balance:

Delivery Progress (DP)
Hit your milestones. Keep the programme moving. Don't let the perfect be the enemy of the good.
Service Quality (SQ)
Genuinely serve user needs. Do the research. Don't release something that will harm, confuse, or exclude people.

There is no win or lose. Both scores will be visible throughout. The final numbers create the conditions for an honest debrief.

Session timing

Allow 90 minutes to 2 hours for a full session. This breaks down as approximately 60–90 minutes for gameplay and 20–30 minutes for debrief. A first run with an unfamiliar facilitator will sit closer to 2 hours; an experienced group can complete gameplay in under an hour.

If time is short, stop at the end of Beta rather than running all four phases; you will still have enough scenario data for a meaningful debrief.

Before you start

Assign a role to each player. Seven roles are available: User Researcher, Content Designer, Interaction Designer, Service Designer, Product Manager, Delivery Manager, and Business Analyst.

Each role has a unique ability, which is shared knowledge — abilities are visible to everyone on the Roles tab. Each role also has a private pressure: a default instinct that reflects how that role tends to behave under real project conditions. Each role also holds a number of tokens to spend each phase — these represent the bandwidth and influence that role has in resolving scenarios. Token allocations are shown on the game board next to each player's name.

Pressures are private. Before dismissing this brief, tell each player their pressure individually — send it to them via a back channel e.g. MS Teams or email. Players should not share their pressure with the group. The tension between ability and pressure is the game, and it works best when pressures are discovered through play, not announced in advance.

The pressures are:

  • User Researcher: "We need more evidence before we proceed."
  • Content Designer: "I wasn't brought in early enough to help."
  • Interaction Designer: "This wasn't in the original brief."
  • Service Designer: "The whole system needs rethinking before we touch this."
  • Product Manager: "We have to hit the milestone. We can fix quality later."
  • Delivery Manager: "We're behind. We need to move, not discuss."
  • Business Analyst: "We need to define the requirements before we can decide."

Facilitator: brief each player on their pressure privately, ensure the pressure info is hidden from view, then share your screen and click "We've read the brief" below to begin.

Game objective

Your team is developing a digital health service during a live outbreak. Players must reach the Review phase having both hit delivery milestones and genuinely served user needs — while navigating the ethical and privacy implications of AI-generated advice drawn from location, contacts, and wearable data.

A high Delivery Progress score with a low Service Quality score is a valid and intentionally uncomfortable outcome. The debrief makes it useful.

There is no win or lose. The scores create the conditions for reflection.

Turn sequence (per scenario)

  1. Reveal the scenario card for the current step on the game board
  2. The facilitator reads it aloud
  3. Each player whose role is triggered states their perspective and plays any relevant ability
  4. The team discusses and agrees a response (2 to 3 minutes)
  5. The facilitator reveals the resolution paths. Click the chosen path to apply its score effects and log the decision.
  6. After completing all scenarios in a phase, click the Event card, resolve it, then click the Phase gate
  7. If the gate is passed, advance to the next phase on the board

Phase gates

Discovery to Alpha
Are we solving the right problem?
Requires at least 2 Service Quality points accumulated. If not met, the team have failed their service assessment and must remediate during the next phase. 1 Research and 1 Design token must be removed from the team's Alpha phase starting allocation.
Alpha to Beta
Is this approach viable?
Requires the team to have spent at least one Research token and one Design token in this phase. If not, the product manager may spend a Delivery token to override (the PM starts the next phase with 1 less Delivery token), but loses 1 SQ.
Beta to Live
Is the service ready for more users?
SQ score must be at least half of DP score. If not, the team may choose to push to Live at the cost of 2 SQ points, or remediate during Live which costs them 1 Research and 1 Design token for the Live phase.
Live to Review
Is quality high enough to launch?
No gate. The team always enters Review. This is intentional. The question is answered by the scores themselves.

Tokens

Each player starts each phase with a token allocation. Tokens represent bandwidth and are spent to resolve scenarios or activate abilities.

Token typeHeld bySpend effect
ResearchUser Researcher (2), Service Designer (1), Business Analyst (1)Required for any resolution that involves user evidence. Spent once per card.
DesignContent Designer (1), Interaction Designer (1), Service Designer (1)Required for usability, content, or design quality resolutions.
DeliveryProduct Manager (2), Delivery Manager (2)Required for unblocking decisions, accelerating progress, or overriding gates.
Tokens reset at the start of each phase. Unused tokens do not carry over. The Delivery Manager's borrowing ability pulls from next phase's allocation. Track this explicitly.

Resolution paths

Each scenario card has two or three resolution paths. The team must agree which path to take before the facilitator reveals the resolution panel. Click the chosen path on the game board to apply its score effects and log the decision.

PathTypical costScore effectWhen to use
Full resolution1 Research + 1 Design token+2 SQ, +1 DPTeam takes time to address root cause collaboratively
Partial resolution1 Delivery token+0 SQ, +2 DPTeam deprioritises quality to hit the milestone
EscalationNo token cost−1 SQ, +1 DPTeam defers the problem and it gets worse
Most scenarios must involve two or three roles to reach full resolution. If only one player speaks, that counts as partial resolution at best. The facilitator can enforce this.

Debrief guide

Allow 20 to 30 minutes. The facilitator does not summarise or interpret. Ask the questions and let the team lead.

Opening question

What moment in the game felt most like something that has actually happened to you?

On role pressure

This is the moment pressures become visible to the group for the first time. Each player: describe the instinct your role gave you. When did it work against the team? When did it help? Was it a fair representation of how that role behaves in practice?

On other roles

What did you learn today about what another role is actually dealing with? What would you do differently to support them on a real project?

On the Business Analyst

When did the Business Analyst's instinct to define before deciding help the team? When did it slow you down?

On the scores

Look at where Delivery Progress and Service Quality diverged. What decision caused the gap? Was it the right call at the time?

Closing question

Name one concrete thing you will do differently on your next project.

Facilitator notes

  • Do not allow a single player to dominate scenario resolution. Name the rule: two or three roles must contribute.
  • The delivery manager and product manager will often default to partial resolution. Let them, then debrief it.
  • The Business Analyst will often want more time to clarify requirements. Watch for whether the team uses this productively or as avoidance.
  • Keep the resolution paths hidden until the team has agreed their approach. Use the toggle on the game board.
  • Aim for 5–7 minutes per scenario. Use the on-screen scenario timer on the game board to keep discussions on pace. If the group stalls, name the time — the pressure to decide is part of the game.
  • Do not intervene in decisions, even clearly bad ones. The discomfort is the learning.
  • After a full run, ask players which scenario they want to replay differently.
  • The session summary above records which resolution path was chosen for each scenario — use it as your debrief data.
